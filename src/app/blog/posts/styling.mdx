## Creating the atmosphere

Coffee begins long before the first sip.

For me, the experience starts with the environment—
the quiet of the morning,
the sound of water heating,
the familiar movement of preparing each tool.

A slow ritual helps me transition into the day.

Sometimes it’s soft music in the background.
Sometimes it’s complete silence.

The goal is simple:
to create a moment where I can fully be present.

## Developing my palate

One of the most exciting parts of this journey is learning how to taste.

At first, coffee was simply “good” or “not good.”

Now, I’m slowly beginning to notice more:

- Bright acidity
- Floral aromas
- Sweet aftertaste
- Notes of citrus, chocolate, or stone fruit
- The subtle differences between origins and processing methods

My palate is still developing,
but every cup adds another reference point.

## Building a personal ritual

derd time is not about chasing perfection.

It’s about creating small moments of intention.

Brewing coffee has become a way to slow down.
To reset.
To appreciate simple details.

Some days the brew is excellent.
Some days it misses the mark.

Both are part of the process.

And maybe that’s the ritual I value most:
showing up,
making time,
and enjoying the experience exactly as it is.
